## Writing rules (all brands)

- Never use an em dash or hyphen as a sentence connector.
- Never use AI language: "certainly!", "gladly!", "great question!".
- Never use filler phrases: "it is important to note that", "in the current context".
- Open with a datum, case, or impact claim, never with a generic formula.
- Cultivated standard in technical and institutional texts; always check https://www.normaculta.com.br/. Direct, human language in network content.

## Long academic and technical writing style (canonical)

Standard to apply in pre-project, article, thesis, dissertation, opinion, memorial, substantial filing, and any long-form text that is not network copy. Calibrated from the v4 review of the [THESIS_REF] pre-project (Jun. 2026).

### 1. Rhythm

Alternate short, medium, and long periods within the same paragraph. Short period carries thesis or point. Medium articulates. Long argues. Never three long sentences in a row; never three short sentences in a row.

### 2. Concrete before abstract

Whenever possible, open the paragraph with a scene, datum, case, or primary source. The theoretical category comes after, to name what has already been seen. Example: the generic email announcing automatic portfolio reevaluation appears before the expression "cybernetic state of exception".

### 3. Authorial voice in first person plural

Use "we sustain", "we propose", "it seems to us", "we take a position", "our reading". Avoid "this work intends", "it will be demonstrated", "it will be analyzed". When there is real doubt, admit it: "we are not sure that", "it remains open", "perhaps".

### 4. Living openings, ending without slogan

The first sentence of the paragraph must never be a formula. Ban: "Given the frame", "Two movements compose", "In the first place", "It is important to highlight that", "In light of the foregoing", "In this sense", "It is worth noting". The end of the paragraph may be sharp, and must not become a tic. If two consecutive paragraphs end in a four-word sentence, rewrite one of them.

### 5. Diversified connectives

Replace "furthermore", "moreover", "likewise", and "in this sense" with transitions that follow from the argument itself. In long text, structure may be signaled by enumeration ("first of all", "once that step is done", "what remains"), with caution: enumerating once per chapter is enough.

### 6. Conscious hedging

In pre-project, opinion, and ongoing research, separate what is a thesis to defend, a hypothesis to test, and a provisional reading. Signal degree of consensus: "majority doctrine", "minority reading", "own position", "still under construction".

### 7. Scene, datum, and argument in the body. Citations in footnotes.

The golden rule of the style separates the body of the text from the referencing machinery. The body holds argument, living scene, and datum, with the law, judgment, and comparative statute mentioned in prose when clarity requires. ABNT citations (author-date or complete) go to the footnote, not the middle of the paragraph. The footnote also carries counterpoint, secondary author, and technical qualification.

Adoption of the **numeric footnote system** as the canonical standard for long-form texts (pre-project, article, thesis, dissertation, chapter, opinion, memorial). NBR 10520 admits both systems, author-date and numeric, and the choice is stylistic. Our preference is the footnote because it preserves paragraph rhythm, reduces the visual noise of parenthetical references, and opens space for critical reading of the cited author.

Practical criterion:

- Every ABNT citation goes to the footnote: direct (with quotes and page) and indirect (paraphrase).
- The body may mention the author ("Edelman described...", "Sarlet sustains...") without inserting a year parenthesis. Year and full reference go in the footnote.
- Law, judgment, and comparative statute may appear in the body with short identification (art. 7, XXVII, of the Constitution; [CASE_REF]; KSchG, § 1). Full reference goes in the footnote on first occurrence.
- The footnote is not merely a reference catalog. It is a space of critical operation: it confirms the use made of the author, makes divergences explicit, and qualifies the reach of the citation.

### 8. Patterns always to remove

Blacklist observed in the v4 review:

- Closed enumeration across three or four consecutive paragraphs ("The first... The second... The third... The fourth...")
- Formulaic openings ("Given the frame", "It is worth highlighting", "It matters to observe", "Two movements compose")
- Neutral-color adjectives that disguise laziness: "with clinical precision", "in a robust way", "widely recognized", "fundamental for"
- Repeated slogans at the end of paragraphs
- Citation without operation: bring Author X, mention the thesis, and continue without using what they said

### 9. Closing checklist

Before delivering long-form text:

1. Reading aloud, is there a stretch that sounds like an AI podcast script? Rewrite.
2. Is there a concrete scene or datum in the first third of the paragraph? If not, insert one or open the paragraph another way.
3. Is there marked authorial voice in at least one sentence per section? If not, mark it.
4. Is there a blacklist pattern (item 8)? Remove it.
5. Does the argument withstand committee critique? Where the thesis is a hypothesis, signal it as a hypothesis.
